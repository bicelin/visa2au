Some immigration changes are announced months ahead, debated, and then land quietly. This one did not. The *Migration Amendment (Onshore Application Charge and Other Measures) Regulations 2026* was made on **26 November 2026**, registered the next day, and commenced on **28 November 2026**. Applicants had roughly a weekend.

If you are one of the families who had been gathering documents for months — police certificates from overseas, health examinations, statements, translations — you already know the feeling of opening ImmiAccount on Saturday and finding a different number.

## What actually changed

A new **Onshore Application Charge of $1,500** has been added as a component of the visa application charge. It is not a new form and not a separate payment step: it is added to the **first instalment** when you lodge, on top of the base application charge and any additional applicant charges.

Two details matter more than the headline figure:

- **It is per person, not per application.** A family of three applying together pays $4,500 in Onshore Application Charges alone.
- **It follows the main applicant's location.** If the main applicant is in Australia when the application is made, a family member included in the same application pays the $1,500 even if that family member is outside Australia.

## The number that changed, for the visa most people ask about

For an onshore **partner visa (subclass 820/801)**, the first instalment moved from **$11,710 to $13,210** for a single applicant. That is the figure a couple preparing a partner application now faces, before health examinations, police checks, translations, and our professional fee.

The Department's own pricing estimator, run with a lodgement date of 28 November 2026, shows the shift across the affected visas:

| Visa (applying in Australia) | Before 28 Nov 2026 | From 28 Nov 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Partner visa 820/801, one applicant | $11,710 | **$13,210** |
| Partner visa 820/801, plus one adult and one child | $20,505 | **$25,005** |
| Student visa 500, higher education, one applicant | $2,500 | **$4,000** |
| Temporary Graduate 485, one applicant | $5,750 | **$7,250** |
| Temporary Graduate 485, plus one adult family member | $8,625 | **$11,625** |
| Child visa 802 or Dependent Child visa 445 | $4,040 | **$5,540** |
| Aged Parent 804, Remaining Relative 835 or Aged Dependent Relative 838 | $6,600 | **$8,100** |
| Contributory Aged Parent 864 or 884 (first instalment) | $6,300 | **$7,800** |
| Carer visa 836 | $2,720 | **$4,220** |

## Which visas the charge attaches to

The regulation lists visa **classes**, not subclass numbers. Read together with the Department's pages, the affected visas are the onshore partner, student, Temporary Graduate, child, parent and other family visas:

- **Partner (Residence) (Class BS)** — the onshore partner visa 820/801
- **Student (Temporary) (Class TU)** — the Student visa 500
- **Skilled (Provisional) (Class VC)** — the Temporary Graduate visa 485, all streams
- **Child (Residence) (Class BT)** — Child visa 802 and Orphan Relative 837
- **Extended Eligibility (Temporary) (Class TK)** — Dependent Child visa 445
- **Aged Parent (Residence) (Class BP)** — Aged Parent visa 804
- **Contributory Aged Parent (Residence) (Class DG)** — Contributory Aged Parent 864
- **Contributory Aged Parent (Temporary) (Class UU)** — Contributory Aged Parent (Temporary) 884
- **Other Family (Residence) (Class BU)** — Remaining Relative 835, Carer 836, Aged Dependent Relative 838

Visas outside this list — employer sponsored, skilled permanent, visitor, and the offshore partner visa 309/100 — do not attract the charge.

## Who does not pay it

Some applicants pay nothing extra, and it is worth checking before you assume the worst:

- **Eligible Pacific Island and Timor-Leste citizens** who receive the lower visa cost concession.
- **ASEAN citizens applying for a Student visa in Australia.** Our own clients from Vietnam, the Philippines, Thailand, Indonesia and Malaysia are commonly in this position.
- **Student visa applicants on an exemption to apply in Australia who are seeking an ELICOS or Non-Award course.**
- **Anyone applying while they — and the main applicant in a combined application — are outside Australia.**
- **Applications lodged before 28 November 2026.** The date that matters is the date the application is made, not the date the visa is decided.

## The 485 is the hard one to avoid

The charge is called an *onshore* charge, but the Department says it applies to **all** Temporary Graduate visa applications. A graduate finishing their studies is almost always in Australia, so there is no offshore route to step around it. For a 485 applicant with a partner and a child who can be included, the charge alone can be $4,500.

## Where we are still being careful

We would rather tell you what is confirmed than fill the gap with confidence. The regulation's table names classes; the Department's own pages confirm the **Student visa 500** and the **Temporary Graduate 485**. The mapping of the **Student Guardian visa 590** and the **820 stage of the partner visa** to those classes is not something we have verified in the Department's published pages, and no exemptions are set out in the regulation text itself. If yours is one of those cases, we will confirm it with the Department before you lodge — not after.

## What to do now

1. **Recalculate before you lodge.** Add $1,500 for every applicant who pays, to any onshore partner, student, 485, child, parent or family application not yet submitted.
2. **Check your passport and stream.** The concessions and exemptions turn on citizenship, the stream and the rate you pay.
3. **Count everyone in the application.** Family members included from outside Australia still pay when the main applicant is in Australia.
4. **Do not lodge in a hurry to save $1,500.** An invalid or rushed application costs far more than the charge. A refusal, a review, and a second lodgement fee dwarf it.
5. **Get the timing and the strategy right first.** For some people a different pathway, or a different place of lodgement, avoids the charge entirely — but moving offshore affects bridging visas and your stay in Australia, so it is not a step to take on a weekend decision.

## If this has landed on you badly

If you had a partner application ready to lodge this week, or a student visa you were about to submit, the extra $1,500 per person is real money — and the way it arrived, with three days' notice, is hard to accept. We understand that.

Nothing in the change makes your documents worthless. Most of what you have gathered is still valid, and the strategy behind your application has not changed. What has changed is the arithmetic, and the timing question of whether an offshore or a different pathway is now better for you.
