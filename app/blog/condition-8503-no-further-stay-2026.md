There is a conversation we have had many times, and since Saturday it has a different answer. A visitor arrives to see family, or on a holiday that keeps getting extended. Three months later there is a partner, or a course they want to start, or an employer who wants them. They ask: *can we just apply from here?*

Until 27 November, often yes. From **28 November 2026**, for anyone granted a visitor visa on or after that date, the answer is **no** — unless the Department waives the condition, and it waives it on narrow grounds.

## What changed on Saturday

Condition **8503 (No Further Stay)** is now **mandatory on every Visitor visa (subclass 600) stream, on the ETA (subclass 601) and on the eVisitor (subclass 651)**. It was already mandatory on the Sponsored Family, Approved Destination Status and Frequent Traveller streams; the regulations extend it to the rest, including the Tourist and Business Visitor streams.

The change comes from the *Migration Amendment (Preventing Further Stay for Visitor Visas) Regulations 2026*, announced by the Minister at the National Press Club in September and commenced on **Saturday 28 November 2026**.

One technical detail decides everything: **it turns on the date you lodge, not the date you arrive or the date the visa is decided.** An application lodged on 27 November is assessed under the old rules even if it is granted in December. An application lodged on or after 28 November carries 8503. If you already hold a visitor visa granted before that date, this does not change it — check your grant letter or VEVO, because the condition was already discretionary and may be there.

## What condition 8503 actually does

A person subject to 8503 **cannot make a valid application, in Australia, for a substantive visa**, unless the Minister waives the condition. The single exception is a **protection visa**.

In practice, for a visitor already in Australia, that means you cannot lodge:

- a **partner visa (820/801)**, even if you have married an Australian citizen or started a de facto relationship
- a **student visa (500)**
- a **Temporary Graduate visa (485)** or a **skilled** application
- an **employer-sponsored** application, even with an employer ready to nominate
- **another visitor visa** to extend the stay you are already on

You may still hold a bridging visa where one applies, and you may still apply for protection if your circumstances genuinely engage Australia's protection obligations — but that is not a workaround to be chosen for convenience.

## The waiver, and what it will not accept

There is one route: a **No Further Stay waiver request**. It is not an extension and not a discretion the Department applies generously. Under the Department's own published guidance, you need **a major change in your situation that happened after your visa was granted and was outside your control**.

Accepted examples:

- unable to travel for a **medical reason**
- **death or serious illness** of a close family member
- a **natural disaster** in your home country
- **war or civil unrest** in your home country
- your **school cannot provide** the approved course

Explicitly **not** accepted examples:

- **marriage or starting a de facto relationship** with an Australian citizen or permanent resident
- **failing your course**
- **pregnancy**

And a line that catches people who were never told: **not knowing the condition was on your visa is not a reason for a waiver.**

Two further realities. Processing is generally **up to 28 days**, longer if medicals or more information are requested, and **a refusal cannot be reviewed by the Administrative Review Tribunal**. Lodging the request does **not** make your stay lawful while it is decided — if your visa expires first you become unlawful, which carries detention and removal, and a three-year bar on returning. Time it properly, or not at all.

## So what can you actually do?

Four honest options, in the order we usually discuss them:

1. **Check whether 8503 is really on your visa.** Read the grant letter, or VEVO. If it is not there, the analysis changes completely and you may be able to lodge onshore after all.
2. **If it is there, and the change in your circumstances is one of the accepted kinds**, prepare a waiver request with real evidence — medical reports, death certificates, government notices about the disaster or conflict, a letter from the provider. That is the only door, and it is evidence-driven.
3. **Plan the offshore application instead.** A partner visa lodged from outside Australia (309/100) is a genuine option, and for many couples it is faster and less risky than an onshore application that cannot be made. The same is true of a student or skilled application lodged offshore. What changes is that you may need to leave Australia, and the timing of that departure matters for your bridging status.
4. **Do not let the visa expire while you decide.** An unlawful non-citizen is in a far worse position than an offshore applicant. If the visa is close to expiring and none of the above is ready, talk to us before the date, not after.

## If this has just landed on your plans

The hardest cases are the ones where nothing dramatic happened. A family came for a wedding and stayed for a birth. A couple decided in March and the visa was granted in June. Under the old discretion, a case officer could look at that and grant the next visa onshore. Under the new rule, the same facts produce a refusal, and the phrase "outside your control" does the work that sympathy used to do.

That is not a reason to give up on the plan — it is a reason to change the route. Offshore applications are not a worse outcome; they are a different sequence, and they can be prepared while you are still lawfully in Australia.

## Questions people are asking this week

**Can I apply for a partner visa on a visitor visa in Australia?** Not if condition 8503 is on your visa — the application would be invalid. Marriage or de facto with an Australian citizen is **not** grounds for a waiver.

**Does 8503 apply to my existing visitor visa?** The change applies to applications lodged on or after 28 November 2026. Check your grant letter or VEVO: 8503 could be imposed on your visa before that date at a case officer's discretion.

**How long does a waiver take?** Generally up to 28 days, longer if medicals or extra information are requested. It does not keep you lawful while it is decided.

**Can I appeal a refused waiver?** No. A decision not to waive 8503 cannot be reviewed by the Administrative Review Tribunal.

**Can I go offshore and apply from there instead?** Yes for visas that can be lodged outside Australia, including the partner visa 309/100 — but leaving Australia affects bridging visas and your current status, so get advice on timing before you book anything.
