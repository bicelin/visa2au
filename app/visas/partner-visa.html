Partner Visa Australia: Which Stream, What It Costs, How Long It Takes

Subclasses 820/801 · 309/100 · 300

The partner visa is how the spouse or de facto partner of an Australian citizen, permanent resident or eligible New Zealand citizen joins them in Australia. There are three ways in, and the right one depends on where you are when you apply — not on which one sounds faster.

Which stream applies to you

The stream is decided by your location and your circumstances at the moment you apply, and getting it wrong is one of the most common — and most avoidable — reasons for refusal.

Can your partner sponsor you?

The sponsoring partner must be an Australian citizen, a permanent resident, or an eligible New Zealand citizen; be 18 or over; and usually be settled in Australia. Sponsorship is a separate application and is assessed in its own right, so the sponsor's history matters as much as the applicant's.

  • Not prohibited from sponsoring — for example by a previous sponsorship within the last five years, or by having been sponsored as a partner themselves
  • Able to meet the sponsorship undertakings, including support obligations
  • Of good character, with police checks where required

A sponsorship that fails takes the visa application with it, which is why we assess the sponsor before drafting anything else.

Is your relationship recognised?

Australian law recognises both marriage and de facto relationships for this visa. Marriage must be valid under Australian law; a de facto relationship must normally be at least 12 months of living together immediately before you apply, unless one of the exceptions applies:

  • The relationship is registered under an Australian state or territory relationship register
  • There are compelling and compassionate circumstances
  • The relationship is a registered de facto relationship under the Migration Regulations

Temporary separation — for work, study or family — does not end a de facto relationship, provided you are not permanently separated. Same-sex couples are assessed on exactly the same basis as any other couple.

The four aspects of the relationship

Decision-makers assess the relationship against four aspects. Strong applications are consistent across all four; weak applications are usually strong on one (often photographs) and silent on the rest.

AspectWhat is being assessedTypical evidence
FinancialShared finances and mutual obligationJoint account and transaction history, shared bills, transfers, joint loans, insurance beneficiaries, wills
HouseholdShared home and daily lifeLease or mortgage in both names, utility accounts, mail to the same address, division of household responsibilities
SocialHow others see the relationshipForm 888 declarations from Australian citizens or residents, invitations, photographs over time, joint travel
CommitmentDepth and duration of the commitmentLength of the relationship and cohabitation, future plans, knowledge of each other's circumstances, correspondence

Volume is not the measure — consistency is. Contradictions between forms, statutory declarations and the timeline are the single most damaging thing we see in refused files.

The two-stage process: 820 then 801

The onshore pathway is one application assessed in two stages. The 820 (temporary) is decided first; the 801 (permanent) is assessed about two years after lodgement, normally without a second application charge. Couples in a long-term relationship — usually three years together, or two years with a child — may have both stages decided together.

  • The 820 generally carries work and study rights and access to Medicare
  • You must continue to be in a genuine relationship at the 801 stage, and tell the Department if circumstances change
  • The offshore 309/100 pathway runs the same way, with the same two stages

Third-party costs sit outside both numbers — health examinations, police checks in each country you have lived in, NAATI translations, and the sponsor's police checks where required.

How long it takes, and what changes it

Published processing times move, so treat any single figure with suspicion — including ours. As a working expectation, most 820 applications take roughly 12–24 months, and the 801 is assessed about two years after lodgement. What actually moves the date:

  • Whether the evidence is complete and consistent at lodgement — files that need several requests for further information lose months
  • Sponsorship approval, which runs in parallel but can hold the case up if the sponsor's history needs explaining
  • Health examinations and overseas police checks, especially where a country is slow to issue clearances
  • Any request under section 56, which has a deadline and does not stop the clock

Current published times are on the Department of Home Affairs processing-time page.

Why partner applications are refused

Refusals are rarely about the strength of the love story. They are about evidence and mechanics:

  • Evidence concentrated in one aspect — usually photographs — with nothing on finances or the household
  • Inconsistent dates, addresses or employment history across the application, statutory declarations and supporting letters
  • A sponsor who is prohibited, or whose previous sponsorship history was not disclosed
  • The wrong stream: an offshore application lodged while onshore, or a 300 lodged by a couple already living together
  • Missing or defective Form 888 declarations, or declarants who are not Australian citizens or permanent residents
  • Health or character issues that were left unaddressed instead of explained

Family violence provisions

If a relationship ends because of family violence, the secondary applicant may still be able to be granted the visa even though the relationship has ended. The provisions exist precisely so that a person is not forced to stay in a violent relationship to keep their immigration status. Evidence requirements are specific and the matter is handled confidentially — we will explain what is needed and what is not before anything is filed.

Partner visa questions, answered

How much does a partner visa cost in total?

Three amounts: the government charge (from $11,710 for the main applicant, $5,855 for a secondary applicant 18 or over, $2,930 for a child under 18), our professional fee of $5,500, and third-party costs such as health examinations, police checks and translations. Our fee is fixed in your engagement letter before work starts.

How long does a partner visa take?

Most temporary partner visas are decided in roughly 12–24 months, and the permanent stage is usually assessed about two years after lodgement. Published times change between streams and countries, so we plan the evidence around the current published range rather than promising a date.

Do we have to be married to apply?

No. A de facto relationship qualifies on the same basis as marriage, provided you have lived together for at least 12 months immediately before applying, or your relationship is registered in an Australian state or territory, or there are compelling circumstances.

How long do we need to have lived together?

Twelve months immediately before the application, unless the relationship is registered, or compelling and compassionate circumstances apply. Time living apart temporarily for work, study or family does not restart the clock.

Can I work while the application is being processed?

Usually yes. The bridging visa that comes with an onshore application generally carries work rights once your current visa ceases, and the 820 itself carries work and study rights. Conditions depend on the visa you held when you applied, so we check yours specifically.

Can I include my children in the application?

Yes — dependent children can be included, and government charges apply per applicant. Tell us about every child, including those from a previous relationship, before we quote, because it changes both the charge and the evidence required.

What happens if the relationship ends before the 801 is decided?

You must tell the Department. If the relationship ends because of family violence, the family violence provisions may still allow the visa to be granted. Otherwise the application is usually withdrawn or refused, and the timing and options depend on your circumstances.

What if the application is refused?

A refusal can be reviewed at the Administrative Review Tribunal within the deadline stated in the decision letter. We assess the merits first and tell you honestly whether review is worth the cost: $5,500 in professional fees plus a $3,496 government fee, 50% refundable if the review succeeds.

Does registering our relationship help?

It removes the 12-month cohabitation requirement, which matters for couples who have not lived together long. It does not reduce the evidence needed to show the relationship is genuine and continuing.

Which stream is faster — onshore or offshore?

There is no reliable difference, and choosing for speed usually backfires. Choose by where you are lawfully able to be at application and at grant, and by whether you meet the de facto threshold or need the prospective marriage route.

Is a de facto partner visa the same as a partner visa?

Yes. The 820/801 partner visa covers both a married spouse and a de facto partner — the same application, the same two stages and the same four evidence tests. Marriage is not required.

What is the difference between the 820 and the 801?

They are two stages of one application. The 820 is temporary and decided first; the 801 is permanent and assessed about two years after lodgement from the same application.

How much does a 309 visa cost?

From $11,710 in government charges for the main applicant plus $5,500 in professional fees. A secondary applicant 18 or over is $5,855 and a child under 18 is $2,930, and third-party costs are separate.

Does my partner have to apply as well?

The sponsor lodges a separate sponsorship application; the visa application belongs to the applicant. Both are assessed, which is why the sponsor's history matters as much as the applicant's evidence.

Can we apply while I am in Australia on a visitor visa?

Sometimes, but it depends on the conditions on your current visa — condition 8503 is the usual blocker — and on holding a substantive visa. We check your specific visa before anything is lodged.

What happens if we apply offshore?

The offshore 309/100 pathway runs the same two stages, but you must be outside Australia when the application is made and when the visa is granted. Coming to Australia first is not a shortcut.
